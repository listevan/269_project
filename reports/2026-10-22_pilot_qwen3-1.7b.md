# Token replay pilot: results of the 2026-10-22 run

Student `Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B`, teacher `Qwen/Qwen3-4B`, MATH-500 levels 3,4,5, run folder `runs/2026-10-22_qwen3-1.7b/`.
The design, the measures and the gates are defined in the notebook's first cell and in the proposal; this file reports
what came out. Every number below is copied from the run's tables, kept in `reports/2026-10-22_pilot_qwen3-1.7b_tables/`.

## Summary

The instrument works: gates 0 to 3 pass with margin, so the rollouts, the scoring and the replays can be trusted. On the
question itself the pilot finds no signal at this size. **A push on OPSA's tokens does not move success in a way this
sample can see**: damage D = -0.0053 [-0.0132, +0.0007]. The share of OPSA's tokens that come out useful, 25%, is what
sampling noise alone gives when every token has U = 0 (null 30% [20%, 39%]), and so is the 33% that come out harmful.
Both signal gates read NOT DETECTED.

Two things do hold. First, a token drawn at random from the same answers is useful on average (U = +0.068
[+0.008, +0.128]), and the whole of that effect sits in near-certain tokens, p > 0.99, inside correct answers
(U = +0.174 on 18 positions): replacing one with a tail token breaks the answer. Random tokens below p = 0.8 have
U = 0.000 together. The gap between OPSA's set and random tokens, -0.096 [-0.174, -0.019], is therefore a statement about
deterministic tokens, not about OPSA's selection. It stays in the tables but is no longer a gate. Second, within OPSA's
set the rarest tokens (p < 0.2, 14 positions) are not hidden good steps: U = -0.009, with 43% coming out harmful against
29% useful. The one band with a visible effect is p from 0.5 to 0.8 (20 positions, U = -0.094, 85% of them in failed
answers), where a push helps.

What OPSA selects is clear and does not depend on replay noise: 59% words, 2% digits, doubt and fork words four times
over-represented, 58% shared with teacher-negative. Teacher-negative behaves like OPSA. NSR's set, every token of a
failed answer, is neutral, and is in effect the random rule restricted to failed answers.

For the proposal's question: on MATH-500 levels 3 to 5, pushing down OPSA's tokens is locally harmless, and at 8 + 8
continuations single tokens cannot be labelled useful or harmful (sign agreement between two kept passes 64%). The
abstract's "share of useful tokens" needs more continuations per position, not more positions.

Gate 1 also checks the software: vLLM's sampled-token log-probabilities and the teacher-forced transformers ones agree
to 0.0082 on average, so the ROCm build computes the same numbers the rules use. Gate 2 says the replays reproduce
the state they start from. Gate 3 says the second kept pass differs from the first by no more than binomial sampling predicts.

Gates 4 and 5 replace the pilot's original gate 4, "OPSA's usefulness differs from random's", which this run met
(p = 0.014) but which fires for structural reasons: random tokens sit at p near 1, where any replacement is
off-distribution, so the contrast measures determinism rather than selection. The new gates look at OPSA's own set:
does a unit push move success (damage), and is the useful share more than noise would produce.

Two independent kept passes at the same positions differ by about what 8 binomial draws predict, so the replay
adds no noise of its own. But the sign of a single position's U agrees between passes only 64% of the time:
**per-token usefulness is noisy at 8 + 8 continuations**, and the conclusions above are about means over positions,
not about individual tokens. The examples below are illustrations, not evidence.

## Share of useful tokens against the noise floor

"share U>0" counts positions whose U came out positive. If every token had U = 0, dealing each position's observed successes back out between the kept and
redrawn branches at random would still make some shares positive; the null columns give that share and its 95% band.
This null conditions on the observed total (a hypergeometric draw), which keeps the band honest where success rates sit
near 0 or 1; fresh binomial draws at the estimated pooled rate understate it there.
The last columns split the spread of U across positions into sampling noise and what is left for real differences between
tokens (sd of true U = sqrt(var(U) - mean binomial variance)).

## Caveats

- **Size.** 64 positions per rule from 18 to 23 problems, 8 + 8 continuations each. The intervals are wide; the pilot
  was sized to see large gaps only, and the one it saw, OPSA against random, is explained by random's near-certain tokens.
- **Per-token noise.** With 8 continuations a side, a single U is a coarse estimate (sign agreement between kept passes
  64%), and every rule's "share U>0" except random's sits inside the band that noise alone produces. Shares are reported,
  not interpreted.
- **Local measure.** U and D look at one position with the rest of the answer resampled from the same model. They say
  nothing about how a training update at one token changes behaviour elsewhere through the shared weights.
- **Mixed problems only.** Positions were drawn from problems the model sometimes solves. On always-solved or
  never-solved problems a token's effect is pinned near 0 and could not show anyway.
- **Software.** vLLM's nightly ROCm build; gate 1 confirms its log-probabilities match transformers'.

## Next steps

The proposal's Plan tab puts the replay in the environments of studies 1 and 2 first and calls the math checkpoints a
secondary setting, and it already says eight continuations per branch cannot label a single token. In that order:

1. **Study 2's premise audit on SUM** (`lime-nlp/Synthetic_Unanswerable_Math`; measurement check 11 of the Plan): stages 1
   and 2 as they are plus a refusal detector, to see whether the tokens that start a refusal fall in OPSA's lowest 20%. The
   audit needs no replay; the decision-level replay (refuse against answer) follows if they do. The doubt-word
   over-representation found here (2.2% against 0.5%) is the hint this would confirm or withdraw.
2. **More continuations at the same positions**, if the per-token share is wanted: 32 kept + 32 redraws and a second kept
   pass on every position, about 2 hours on the RX 9070 XT. Copy the run folder to a new id, delete `config.json`,
   `positions.jsonl`, `replays.jsonl`, `replay.meta.json` and `tables/`, then
   `uv run run_pilot.py --run-id <new id> --set kept=32 --set redraws=32 --set dup_fraction=1.0`; stage 1 is skipped,
   stage 2 keeps `scores.jsonl` and redraws the positions, which are seeded independently of the redraw count, so the
   same tokens are replayed. Per-position SE falls from about 0.25 to 0.12. Gate: sign agreement between kept passes above 0.85.
3. **The released OPSA checkpoint** `Tuwhy/Qwen3-1.7B-OPSA` through the same pipeline: does training move which tokens are
   low-probability, and does its new lowest 20% hold more doubt words? Gate at stage 1 on truncation; the checkpoint's
   answers run several times longer than the base model's.
4. MBPP+ (sandboxed tests) and the Plan's local update probes after these.
